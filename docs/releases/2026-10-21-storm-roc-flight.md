# Storm Roc: heavier, more natural movement

- The Roc's hop between spires is one complete motion: it crouches and turns on its spire,
  pushes off with one huge downbeat as its legs stream back, beats again, then glides to the
  next spire.
- It no longer swings its body round in midair to face the arena as it arrives. Like a real
  bird, it flies out to the side of the spire, banks round in one wide turn and comes in on a
  straight run, landing already facing you. It banks deeply through the turn, holding its head
  level and looking into the turn, and its talons reach out for the spire well before it lands.
  Its takeoff from the brawl and its descent in the phase change fly past their spire and round
  onto it the same way. Each spire visit is 1.4 seconds longer (with the same number of calls),
  the takeoff 1.9 seconds longer and the phase change 2.4 seconds longer.
- It lands like a great eagle, slowly and heavily for its size: it flares well before the
  spire, pitching up with its talons reaching forward, brakes with one wingbeat and slams onto
  the rock, sinking deep under its weight. It holds its wings high to find its balance, folds
  them slowly and turns to glare at you before its call.
- It no longer dives the instant it touches down: a dive waits for the landing to settle.
- Its lightning call is reworked: its head jerks up to the sky in electric stutters while its
  body swells slowly, the bolt jolts through it, and it opens its wings, crackling at the crest
  and wing edges, before whipping its head down onto you as the first strike lands.
- Its wings fold and open as a bird's do, with no feathers flipping over.
- Its takeoff from the brawl is a slow, heavy launch: it crouches as its wings open, drives off
  the rock as its downbeat blasts you back, and climbs at the spire hop's pace.
- Its dive starts with a crouch on its spire and ends with a high, wide flare just before it
  strikes. Stuck, it is stunned, heaves and wrenches against its buried bill, then gathers
  itself, crackling, and rips free at 5 seconds. The chain smash's hops tear free with a
  screech and one slow wingbeat.
- Its longer flight, seen in the phase change, has two unequal, deliberate wingbeats, a slower
  recovery and a long glide. Its body rises after each push, while its feathers and tail follow through.
- Its head searches the ground in held glances with deliberate turns and sometimes watches a player below.
- The legs stay tucked smoothly through the flight loop, including its first few ticks.
- Small wing and tail adjustments keep the glide alive between strokes.
